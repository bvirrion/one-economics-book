\begin{solution}{exo:g8:competition-and-monopoly-first-look:10}
(a) $(1\,000\,000 + 0.50 \times 2\,000\,000) \div 2\,000\,000 = 2\,000\,000 \div 2\,000\,000 = 1.00$
dalen per cubic metre. (b) Each \omterm{def:g7:firms:firm}{firm}: $(1\,000\,000 + 0.50 \times 1\,000\,000) \div 1\,000\,000 =

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:competition-and-monopoly-first-look:11}
A messaging service is useful because of the people one can reach with it (a \omterm{def:g8:competition-and-monopoly-first-look:network-effect}{network effect}). A user who
moves alone to the new service can reach almost nobody, so each user waits for the others; the large
service keeps its users even if the new one is as good.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:competition-and-monopoly-first-look:12}
What is bought for the \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price} has changed: a ``call'' of 2025 includes more. If the \omterm{def:g8:inflation-and-price-indices:price-index}{price index} does not
fully count this, the real \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price} of the same service has fallen even more than the chart shows; if it
counts it, part of the fall comes from better products, not only from lower \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{prices} for the same thing.
Either way, the chart compares services that are not exactly the same.
\end{solution}
