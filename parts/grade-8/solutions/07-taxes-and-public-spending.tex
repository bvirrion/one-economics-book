\begin{solution}{exo:g8:taxes-and-public-spending:8}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:taxes-and-public-spending:9}
It matches the ``breaks'' line on evasion: people may buy cigarettes abroad or from sellers who do not
pay the \omterm{def:g8:taxes-and-public-spending:tax}{tax}, so recorded sales fall while smoking hardly changes.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:taxes-and-public-spending:10}
2025: $236\,818 \div 393\,264 \approx 60.2\,\%$. After two years: debt $236\,818 + 2 \times 8\,000 =
252\,818$; GDP $393\,264 \times 1.04^{2} \approx 425\,354$; share $252\,818 \div 425\,354 \approx
59.4\,\%$. The share fell, although the debt rose: GDP grew faster than the debt.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:taxes-and-public-spending:11}
(a) $2\,800 \times 0.10 = 280$ dalens. (b) Before: $3\,000$ dalens; after: $2\,800 + 280 = 3\,080$
dalens. (c) The worker bears $3\,000 - 2\,800 = 200$ dalens through the lower \omterm{def:g7:households:wage}{wage}; the employer
bears $3\,080 - 3\,000 = 80$ dalens.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:taxes-and-public-spending:12}
The chart shows how much was spent, not what the \omterm{def:g7:money:money}{money} bought or what it changed: whether children
learned more, people lived longer or roads were better. The same spending can achieve a lot or little;
measuring that needs other data, and comparisons of what happened with and without the spending.
\end{solution}
