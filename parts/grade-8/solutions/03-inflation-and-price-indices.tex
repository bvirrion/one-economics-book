\begin{solution}{exo:g8:inflation-and-price-indices:9}
For example: when a good becomes dearer, \omterm{def:g7:households:household}{households} buy less of it and more of something else, but
the fixed \omterm{def:g8:inflation-and-price-indices:basket}{basket} keeps the old quantities; new goods, and better versions of old goods, enter the
\omterm{def:g8:inflation-and-price-indices:basket}{basket} late or are counted as \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price} rises although they are better.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:inflation-and-price-indices:10}
(a) $393\,264 \div 183\,201 \approx 2.15$ times. (b) 2005: $183\,201 \div 84.6 \times 100 \approx
216\,550$ million; 2025: $393\,264 \div 134.5 \times 100 \approx 292\,390$ million 2015 dalens.
(c) $(292\,390 - 216\,550) \div 216\,550 = 75\,840 \div 216\,550 \approx 0.350$: $35.0\,\%$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:inflation-and-price-indices:11}
(a) About $1.55 \times 10^{11}$ times: eleven powers of ten. (b) $1.55 \times 10^{11} \div 146

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:inflation-and-price-indices:12}
The index weights goods as the average \omterm{def:g7:households:household}{household} buys them. A family that spends more of its \omterm{def:g7:households:budget}{budget}
on goods whose \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{prices} rose fast (energy, for example) faced a higher \omterm{def:g8:inflation-and-price-indices:inflation-rate}{inflation rate}; one that spends
more on goods whose \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{prices} rose slowly, or that owns its home and pays no rent, may have faced a lower
one.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g8:inflation-and-price-indices:1}
\textbf{1.} Bread $300 \times 2.00 = 600$; rent $12 \times 700 = 8\,400$; transport $100 \times 3.00 =
300$; clothing $20 \times 40 = 800$; energy $3\,000 \times 0.25 = 750$ dalens.
\textbf{2.} $600 + 8\,400 + 300 + 800 + 750 = 10\,850$ dalens.
\textbf{3.} $8\,400 \div 10\,850 \approx 77.4\,\%$.
\textbf{4.} Bread $600 \div 10\,850 \approx 5.5\,\%$; transport $\approx 2.8\,\%$; clothing $\approx
7.4\,\%$; energy $\approx 6.9\,\%$.
\textbf{5.} $813 + 12\,001.68 + 391 + 980 + 1\,380 = 15\,565.68$ dalens.
\textbf{6.} $15\,565.68 \div 10\,850 \times 100 \approx 143.5$.
\textbf{7.} Bread $2.71 \div 2.00 = 1.355$: $+35.5\,\%$; rent $1\,000.14 \div 700 \approx 1.429$:
$+42.9\,\%$; transport $\approx +30.3\,\%$; clothing $+22.5\,\%$; energy $0.46 \div 0.25 = 1.84$:
$+84.0\,\%$.
\textbf{8.} Rent weighs $77.4\,\%$ of the \omterm{def:g8:inflation-and-price-indices:basket}{basket} and energy only $6.9\,\%$: the index is a weighted
average, so it follows rent closely.
\textbf{9.} $(143.5 - 140.0) \div 140.0 = 0.025$: $2.5\,\%$.
\textbf{10.} $(3\,600 - 2\,400) \div 2\,400 = 0.5$: $50\,\%$.
\textbf{11.} $3\,600 \div 143.5 \times 100 \approx 2\,509$ dalens of 2015.
\textbf{12.} 2015: $600 + 300 + 800 + 750 = 2\,450$ dalens. 2025: $813 + 391 + 980 + 1\,380 = 3\,564$
dalens. $3\,564 \div 2\,450 \approx 1.455$: a rise of $45.5\,\%$, more than the index.
\textbf{13.} $(2\,509 - 2\,400) \div 2\,400 = 109 \div 2\,400 \approx 0.045$: the \omterm{def:g8:inflation-and-price-indices:real-wage}{real wage} rose by
about $4.5\,\%$.
\end{solution}
