\begin{solution}{exo:g8:production-and-productivity:3}

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:production-and-productivity:6}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:production-and-productivity:7}
Before: $1\,800 \times 50 = 90\,000$ dalens. After: $1\,700 \times 54 = 91\,800$ dalens. It rose,

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:production-and-productivity:8}
Qatar: its \omterm{def:g6:living-standards:income-per-person}{income per person} ($127\,100$ dollars) is more than twice Germany's, with a lower output
per hour. Its workers work many hours ($2\,435$ a year), a large share of its residents work
($69.1\,\%$), and it earns from the gas it sells abroad. (Norway, with its oil, is another answer.)
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:production-and-productivity:9}
For example: many countries with a high output per hour but a low \omterm{def:g6:living-standards:income-per-person}{income per person}, with no
difference in hours, in the share of people working or in resources to explain it; or countries
with much more \omterm{def:g8:production-and-productivity:capital-per-worker}{capital per worker} producing no more per hour.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:production-and-productivity:10}
$(600 \times 20 + 400 \times 50) \div 1\,000 = (12\,000 + 20\,000) \div 1\,000 = 32$ dalens an hour.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:production-and-productivity:11}
$(500 \times 20 + 500 \times 50) \div 1\,000 = (10\,000 + 25\,000) \div 1\,000 = 35$ dalens an hour.
The average rose because a larger share of workers is now in the job where an hour produces more:
the weights of the average changed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:production-and-productivity:12}
The chart shows that the two go together. Higher productivity may raise \omterm{def:g7:households:income}{incomes}; higher \omterm{def:g7:households:income}{incomes} may
also pay for schools and machines that raise productivity; and a third thing, such as the rules
under which people work and trade, may raise both. A chart of one year cannot tell these apart.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g8:production-and-productivity:1}
\textbf{1.} $629 + 1\,986 + 5\,164 = 7\,779$ workers.
\textbf{2.} Farming $65\,693 \div 629 \approx 104.4$; manufacturing $188\,438 \div 1\,986 \approx
94.9$; services $361\,526 \div 5\,164 \approx 70.0$ thousand dalens.
\textbf{3.} Output $65\,693 + 188\,438 + 361\,526 = 615\,657$; $615\,657 \div 7\,779 \approx 79.1$
thousand dalens.
\textbf{4.} Farming.
\textbf{5.} Farming $118\,157 \div 629 \approx 187.8$; manufacturing $312\,697 \div 1\,986 \approx
157.5$; services $326\,637 \div 5\,164 \approx 63.3$ thousand dalens.
\textbf{6.} \omterm{def:g8:production-and-productivity:capital}{Capital} $118\,157 + 312\,697 + 326\,637 = 757\,491$; $757\,491 \div 7\,779 \approx 97.4$
thousand dalens.
\textbf{7.} By \omterm{def:g8:production-and-productivity:capital-per-worker}{capital per worker}: farming, manufacturing, services. By \omterm{def:g7:specialisation:output-per-worker}{output per worker}: farming,
manufacturing, services. The same order.
\textbf{8.} \omterm{def:g8:production-and-productivity:capital}{Capital}: $187.8 \div 63.3 \approx 3.0$ times. Output: $104.4 \div 70.0 \approx 1.5$
times. \omterm{def:g7:specialisation:output-per-worker}{Output per worker} differs much less than \omterm{def:g8:production-and-productivity:capital-per-worker}{capital per worker}.
\textbf{9.} No. Farm workers work with about three times as much capital each; the census measures
output, not effort.
\textbf{10.} Manufacturing $1\,986 + 500 = 2\,486$; services $5\,164 - 500 = 4\,664$.
\textbf{11.} Manufacturing $2\,486 \times 94.9 = 235\,921.4$; services $4\,664 \times 70.0 =
326\,480$ thousand dalens.
\textbf{12.} $(65\,693 + 235\,921.4 + 326\,480) \div 7\,779 = 628\,094.4 \div 7\,779 \approx 80.7$
thousand dalens, up from $79.1$.
\textbf{13.} \omterm{def:g8:production-and-productivity:capital}{Capital}: manufacturing works with about $157.5$ thousand dalens of \omterm{def:g8:production-and-productivity:capital-per-worker}{capital per worker},
so the 500 workers need about $500 \times 157.5 = 78\,750$ thousand dalens of machines and buildings,
paid for by someone's \omterm{def:g7:households:saving}{saving}; and they need the skills and methods of factory work.
\end{solution}
