\begin{solution}{exo:g9:exchange-rates-and-purchasing-power:9}
$500 \times 1.25 = 625$ dalens, against 800 in Brenland: a gap of 175 dalens, so the observation contradicts
the law of one \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price}. For example: shipping a television costs \omterm{def:g7:money:money}{money}; Brenland may have a \omterm{def:g9:international-trade:tariff}{tariff} or a
higher \omterm{def:g8:taxes-and-public-spending:tax}{tax} on televisions; buyers may not know the Kestrian \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price}, or the guarantee may not hold abroad.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:exchange-rates-and-purchasing-power:10}
At the \omterm{def:g7:markets-and-prices:market}{market} rate: $2\,000\,000 \div 100 = 20\,000$ dollars. At the PPP rate: $2\,000\,000 \div 40 =
50\,000$ dollars. The PPP figure says more about what people can buy, because it uses the \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{prices} they
actually pay.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:exchange-rates-and-purchasing-power:11}
A haircut cannot be shipped: it is made where it is bought, and its \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price} depends on the \omterm{def:g7:households:wage}{wages} of local
hairdressers, which are lower in a poorer country. A phone can be shipped: if it were much cheaper in one
country, traders would buy it there and sell it elsewhere, so its converted \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price} is about the same.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:exchange-rates-and-purchasing-power:12}
It overstates the gap. \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{Prices}, services above all, are lower in poor countries (the price-level chart), so
their \omterm{def:g7:households:income}{incomes} buy more than the \omterm{def:g7:markets-and-prices:market}{market} rate suggests. India's \omterm{def:g8:gdp:gdp-per-person}{GDP per person} is a thirty-third of the
United States' at \omterm{def:g7:markets-and-prices:market}{market} rates but an eighth at purchasing-power parity.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g9:exchange-rates-and-purchasing-power:1}
\textbf{1.} $2\,484 \div 1.242 = 2\,000$ kests.
\textbf{2.} $30 \times 1.242 = 37.26$ dalens.
\textbf{3.} $15 \times 1.242 = 18.63$ dalens.
\textbf{4.} The kest became dearer, so the dalen depreciated.
\textbf{5.} $(1.242 - 1.207) \div 1.207 = 2.9\,\%$.
\textbf{6.} 2024: $2\,000 \times 1.207 = 2\,414$ dalens; 2025: $2\,000 \times 1.242 = 2\,484$ dalens.
\textbf{7.} $60 \div 40 = 1.5$ dalens per kest.
\textbf{8.} $40 \times 1.242 = 49.68$ dalens: the \omterm{def:g8:inflation-and-price-indices:basket}{basket} is cheaper in Kestria.
\textbf{9.} $49.68 \div 60 = 0.828$: $82.8\,\%$.
\textbf{10.} $30\,000 \times 1.242 = 37\,260$ dalens.
\textbf{11.} $30\,000 \times 1.5 = 45\,000$ dalens.
\textbf{12.} At the \omterm{def:g7:markets-and-prices:market}{market} rate, Brenland is richer per person ($42\,327$ against $37\,260$ dalens); at the
PPP rate, Kestria is ($45\,000$ against $42\,327$). To compare what people can buy, one uses the PPP rate,
because \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{prices} are lower in Kestria.
\end{solution}
