\begin{solution}{exo:g7:firms:11}
The new bakeries add to the bread on sale: the \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price} of bread falls, and each bakery sells less.
The \omterm{def:g7:firms:profit}{profit} of each bakery falls. New bakeries stop opening when \omterm{def:g7:firms:profit}{profits} are no higher than what
the owners could earn elsewhere; if \omterm{def:g7:firms:profit}{profits} turn into losses, some bakeries close. The number of
bakeries rises, then settles.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g7:firms:1}
\textbf{1.} \omterm{def:g7:firms:fixed-cost}{Fixed cost} 600 dalens a month; \omterm{def:g7:firms:variable-cost}{variable cost} $0.90 + 0.10 = 1.00$ dalen per cup.
\textbf{2.} $600 + 1.00 \times 500 = 1\,100$ dalens.
\textbf{3.} The \omterm{def:g7:firms:fixed-cost}{fixed cost} of 600 dalens (the stall and the blender).
\textbf{4.} $1\,100 \div 500 = 2.20$ dalens per cup.
\textbf{5.} $2.50 \times 600 = 1\,500$; $3 \times 500 = 1\,500$; $4 \times 250 = 1\,000$ dalens.
\textbf{6.} $600 + 600 = 1\,200$; $600 + 500 = 1\,100$; $600 + 250 = 850$ dalens.
\textbf{7.} $1\,500 - 1\,200 = 300$; $1\,500 - 1\,100 = 400$; $1\,000 - 850 = 150$ dalens. The \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price}
of 3 dalens gave the largest \omterm{def:g7:firms:profit}{profit}.
\textbf{8.} Each cup leaves $3 - 1 = 2$ dalens: $600 \div 2 = 300$ cups.
\textbf{9.} \omterm{def:g7:firms:revenue}{Revenue} $3 \times 280 = 840$, \omterm{def:g7:firms:total-cost}{total cost} $600 + 280 = 880$: a \omterm{def:g7:firms:loss}{loss} of 40 dalens.
\textbf{10.} \omterm{def:g7:firms:revenue}{Revenue} $2.50 \times 420 = 1\,050$, \omterm{def:g7:firms:total-cost}{total cost} $600 + 420 = 1\,020$: a \omterm{def:g7:firms:profit}{profit} of 30
dalens.
\textbf{11.} Each cup leaves $2.50 - 1 = 1.50$ dalens: $600 \div 1.50 = 400$ cups.
\textbf{12.} The second stall already pushed Mira's \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price} down and her \omterm{def:g7:firms:profit}{profit} from 400 to 30 dalens.
A third stall would take more customers: at $2.50$ dalens she would sell fewer than 420 cups and
could fall below her \omterm{def:g7:firms:break-even}{break-even quantity} of 400. The model predicts lower \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{prices} and \omterm{def:g7:firms:profit}{profits}; if
the stalls make losses, one of them closes.
\end{solution}
