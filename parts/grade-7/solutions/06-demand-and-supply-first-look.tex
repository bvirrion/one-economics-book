\begin{solution}{exo:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:4}

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:5}
A \omterm{def:g7:markets-and-prices:market-surplus}{market surplus} of $70 - 30 = 40$ kg. Sellers left with plums lower their \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price}, towards

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:6}
(a) The \omterm{def:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:demand-curve}{demand curve} \omterm{def:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:shift}{shifts} to the right. (b) The \omterm{def:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:equilibrium-price}{equilibrium price} and the \omterm{def:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:equilibrium-quantity}{equilibrium
quantity} both rise.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:7}
(a) The \omterm{def:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:supply-curve}{supply curve} \omterm{def:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:shift}{shifts} to the right. (b) The \omterm{def:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:equilibrium-price}{equilibrium price} falls and the \omterm{def:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:equilibrium-quantity}{equilibrium
quantity} rises.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:8}
4 dalens and 800 baskets. The \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price} rose by $4 - 3 = 1$ dalen and the quantity by
$800 - 600 = 200$ baskets.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:9}
The picture predicts a higher \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price} and a smaller quantity sold. A fall in the \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price}, or a
rise in the quantity sold, with nothing else changed, would contradict it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:10}
(a) At 2, 3, 4 and 5 dalens: 130, 110, 90 and 70 kg. (b) At 5 dalens, 70 kg are demanded and
70 kg supplied: the new equilibrium is 5 dalens and 70 kg. (c) Both rose: the \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price} from 4 to
5 dalens, the quantity from 50 to 70 kg.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:11}
(a) The quantity rises: each \omterm{def:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:shift}{shift} alone raises it. (b) The \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price} is uncertain: the demand
\omterm{def:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:shift}{shift} raises it and the supply \omterm{def:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:shift}{shift} lowers it. If demand moves more, it rises; if supply
moves more, it falls; it can stay the same. Sketch one case where each happens.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:12}
A higher \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price} can come from a \omterm{def:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:demand-curve}{demand curve} moving right or from a \omterm{def:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:supply-curve}{supply curve} moving left.
The first raises the quantity sold, the second lowers it. Knowing the quantities sold each
month, as well as the \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{prices}, would help tell the two apart.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:1}
\textbf{1.} 900 baskets.
\textbf{2.} 700 baskets.
\textbf{3.} A \omterm{def:g7:markets-and-prices:shortage}{shortage}: $800 - 400 = 400$ baskets.
\textbf{4.} A \omterm{def:g7:markets-and-prices:market-surplus}{market surplus}: $800 - 400 = 400$ baskets.
\textbf{5.} The \omterm{def:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:demand-curve}{demand curve} joins $(1\,000, 2)$ to $(400, 8)$, going down; the \omterm{def:g7:demand-and-supply-first-look:supply-curve}{supply curve}
joins $(200, 2)$ to $(800, 8)$, going up.
\textbf{6.} They cross at 6 dalens and 600 baskets; in the table, 600 baskets are demanded and
600 supplied at 6 dalens.
\textbf{7.} $6 \times 600 = 3\,600$ dalens a week.
\textbf{8.} At 7 dalens, 700 baskets are supplied and 500 demanded: a \omterm{def:g7:markets-and-prices:market-surplus}{market surplus} of 200
baskets. Growers with unsold baskets lower the \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price}.
\textbf{9.} 400, 600, 800, 1\,000, 1\,200, 1\,400 and 1\,600 baskets at 2 to 8 dalens.
\textbf{10.} At 4 dalens, 800 baskets are demanded and 800 supplied: 4 dalens and 800 baskets.
\textbf{11.} At 6 dalens, 1\,200 baskets would be supplied and 600 demanded: a \omterm{def:g7:markets-and-prices:market-surplus}{market surplus}
of 600 baskets, so the \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{price} would fall.
\textbf{12.} \omterm{def:g7:money:price}{Price}: $(4 - 6) \div 6 \approx -0.333$, a fall of $33.3\,\%$. Quantity:
$(800 - 600) \div 600 \approx 0.333$, a rise of $33.3\,\%$. Spending: $4 \times 800 = 3\,200$
dalens a week, against $3\,600$ in May.
\end{solution}
