\begin{solution}{exo:b3:platforms-and-innovation:2}
$P_0 = 0.5 \times 10 + 0.3 \times 20 + 0.2 \times 5 = 12$ and $P_1 = 0.5 \times 11 + 0.3 \times 20 + 0.2 \times 30 = 17.5$: a change of 5.5. Within $0.5 \times 1 = 0.5$; between and
cross 0, since the shares of continuing firms do not change; entry $0.2 \times (30 - 12) = 3.6$; exit
$-0.2 \times (5 - 12) = 1.4$. The parts sum to 5.5, and net entry makes up $5.0/5.5 = 91~\%$ of it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:platforms-and-innovation:5}
Profit $(2 + n_2 - n_1)n_1 + (2 - n_2)n_2$. The conditions $2 + n_2 - 2n_1 = 0$ and $n_1 + 2 - 2n_2 = 0$ give $n_1 = n_2 = 2$; fees $p_1 = 3 + 2 - 2 = 3$
and $p_2 = 3 - 2 = 1$. By the rule, $p_1 = 1 + 2 - 0 = 3$ and $p_2 = 1 + 2 - 1 \times 2 = 1$: side 2 pays only its cost, because its members
bring value to side 1.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:platforms-and-innovation:6}

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:platforms-and-innovation:7}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:platforms-and-innovation:10}
The proof is in the text. Side $i$'s fee $f_i + n_i - \alpha_j n_j$ is negative when $\alpha_j n_j > f_i + n_i$: when the other side values
its members enough, the platform pays them to join.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:platforms-and-innovation:11}
The inventor's condition $\tfrac14 A/\sqrt R = 1$ gives $\sqrt R = A/4$. Welfare is the holder's half during the \omterm{def:b3:platforms-and-innovation:patent}{patent}
plus the full saving after it, less research: $W = \sqrt R(\tfrac12 A + 20 - A) - R = 5A - 3A^2/16$. It peaks at $A = 40/3$: $1 - e^{-0.05T} = \tfrac23$, so
$T = 20\ln 3 = 22$ years, with research of $(40/3)^2/16 = 11.1$ million.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:platforms-and-innovation:ride}
% figdata: ride_stats
\textbf{1.} $10n(1 - n) = 1.6$: $n = 0.2$ or 0.8, besides 0.

\textbf{2.} The lower share: 2{,}000 residents.

\textbf{3.} With 1{,}000 users the marginal resident values it at $10 \times 0.9 \times 0.1 = 0.9$, below the price: use shrinks to
zero.

\textbf{4.} $10/4 = 2.5$ dalens a month.

\textbf{5.} Enough free months to bring at least 1{,}000 more residents in, to pass 2{,}000; then use
grows on its own towards 8{,}000.

\textbf{6.} With no users the service is worth nothing to anyone, so no one pays to join.

\textbf{7.} $(3 + 1.2n_D - n_R)n_R + (1 + 0.4n_R - n_D)n_D$.

\textbf{8.} $3 + 1.6n_D - 2n_R = 0$ and $1 + 1.6n_R - 2n_D = 0$.

\textbf{9.} $n_R = 7.6/1.44 = 5.278$ and $n_D = 6.8/1.44 = 4.722$ hundred.

\textbf{10.} $p_R = 4 + 1.2 \times 4.722 - 5.278 = 4.389$; $p_D = 2 + 0.4 \times 5.278 - 4.722 = -0.611$: drivers are paid 0.61 dalens a month.

\textbf{11.} $1 + 5.278 - 0.4 \times 4.722 = 4.389$ and $1 + 4.722 - 1.2 \times 5.278 = -0.611$.

\textbf{12.} Drivers: each hundred of them is worth 1.2 to every rider, three times what riders are
worth to drivers.

\textbf{13.} $3.389 \times 5.278 - 1.611 \times 4.722 = 10.28$ hundred dalens a month.

\textbf{14.} It rises: the conditions become $2n_R - 1.2n_D = 3$ and $2n_D - 1.2n_R = 1$, so $n_R = 2.81$, $n_D = 2.19$ and $p_D = 1 + 2.19 - 0.8 \times 2.81 = 0.94$.

\textbf{15.} On riders, who use one platform: each platform is the only way for drivers to
reach its riders.

\textbf{16.} It rises: a platform can charge drivers for access to riders whom no rival can
deliver to them.

\textbf{17.} Riders find the same drivers on both platforms, so the larger one's network
advantage shrinks.

\textbf{18.} The value of the incumbent's drivers to a rider, $1.2 \times 4.722 = 5.67$ dalens a month, beyond matching its fee.

\textbf{19.} 70.01 and 73.66 thousand dalens per worker: growth of 3.65, or 5.2~\%.

\textbf{20.} Within 1.30, between 0.08, cross 0.06, entry 0.38, exit 1.83.

\textbf{21.} $(0.38 + 1.83)/3.65 = 60.5~\%$.

\textbf{22.} The exit term removes employment at productivity far below the mean; each
worker leaving such a firm raises the average by the full gap.

\textbf{23.} Like U.K. manufacturing's labour productivity (50~\%), and short of U.S. retail, where
net entry was virtually all of it.

\textbf{24.} Riders pay 4.39 dalens a month and drivers are paid 0.61; entry and exit account for
60.5~\% of the services sector's productivity growth.
\end{solution}
