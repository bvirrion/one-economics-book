\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g6:a-connected-world:1}
\textbf{1.} 2000: $(48\,640 + 48\,918) \div 152\,000 = 97\,558 \div 152\,000 \approx
64.2\,\%$. 2025: $(129\,777 + 128\,664) \div 393\,264 = 258\,441 \div 393\,264 \approx
65.7\,\%$.
\textbf{2.} $40\,231 \div 129\,777 \approx 31.0\,\%$.
\textbf{3.} Yes: $40\,231 - 34\,739 = 5\,492$ million dalens more.
\textbf{4.} \omterm{def:g6:a-connected-world:export}{Exports} and \omterm{def:g6:a-connected-world:import}{imports} grew about as fast as output, so their share of output
stayed almost the same.
\textbf{5.} $4\,000 \div 20 = 200$ dalens a tonne in 1970; $1\,600 \div 20 = 80$ dalens
in 2025.
\textbf{6.} $(4\,000 - 1\,600) \div 4\,000 = 2\,400 \div 4\,000 = 0.6$: a fall of
$60\,\%$.
\textbf{7.} $1\,600 \div 200 = 8$ dalens per bicycle; $8 \div 300 \approx 0.027$, about
$2.7\,\%$ of the price.
\textbf{8.} $30 \div 3 = 10$ dalens a minute, so ten minutes cost $100$ dalens.
\textbf{9.} 2009: $1\,000$; 2013: $500$; 2017: $250$; 2021: $125$; 2025: $62.5$
dalens.
\textbf{10.} $2\,000 \div 62.5 = 32$ times cheaper (five halvings).
\textbf{11.} Yes: every four years the price is divided by the same number, 2, and on a
\omterm{def:g6:reading-economic-charts:times-ten-scale}{times-ten scale} equal divisions are equal steps, so the points lie on a straight line
going down.
\textbf{12.} For example: ``Shipping now adds only a few dalens to the price of a
Kestrian bicycle, so Brenland's shops can sell it at a price close to Kestria's. Free
calls let a shop owner order, check and complain every day at no cost.''
\end{solution}
