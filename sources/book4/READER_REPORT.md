## Overall judgement

**Part I, Uncertainty and time (chapters 1--5).** Sound and well taught. Every solution recomputed matches its
printed answer except a handful of rounding-path nits (R4-004, R4-005). Each model box is honest, and the
behavioural chapter presents replication failures as evidence, not as a camp. The defects are measurement
mismatches: a marginal load compared with a premium load (R4-001); two different endpoints in one ratio (R4-002);
Social Security contributions called saving (R4-003). No chapter is thin.

**Part II, Equilibrium and welfare (chapters 6--9).** Rigorous: the proofs of the first welfare theorem, the
Arrow sketch and the median-voter theorem check out step by step. Market failure and the limits of correcting it
sit side by side (ch. 7, l. 322--326), and planned and market prices are treated symmetrically (ch. 8,
l. 448--451). Defects:
- one wrong derived percentage carried from the ledger (R4-007, the "23 per cent" fall in freight);
- the track take counted as longshot bias (R4-008);
- two model-box "tested" fields that cite activity, not results (R4-009, R4-010).
No chapter is thin.

**Part III, Games (chapters 10--14).** The strongest part. Every exercise and problem answer recomputes exactly,
including the root-found cement price (119.07) and the licence-auction standard deviations. Every listing does what
the prose says. The opening charts are faithful to their ledger rows; the auction slopes were rebuilt from the
published risk-aversion estimates. Defects are a wrong sample-size claim (R4-012), unstated sample selection in one
chart (R4-016) and wording slips in two proofs (R4-013, R4-014). No chapter is thin.

**Part IV, Information (chapters 15--18).** Clear and well paced, and the numbers almost all recompute. The
government-failure section of ch. 18 and the costs of each answer to adverse selection (ch. 15, l. 265--271) are
balanced, as the brief asks. The weak spots:
- one wrong exercise solution: the Rothschild--Stiglitz threshold uses full pooled cover only (R4-020, major);
- two "tested" fields that cite consistency rather than a test of the model (R4-021, R4-023);
- a text/chart mismatch on the 2005 allowance price (R4-022).
No chapter is thin.

**Part V, Dynamics and macroeconomics (chapters 19--29).** The strongest part of the book for the mathematics. Every
model solution I recomputed matches: precautionary saving, q theory, OLG with PAYG, the Ramsey eigenvalues, the
semi-endogenous logistic, DMP, the inflation-bias problem with its optimal weight, the three-equation path, the analytic
RBC model and Diamond--Dybvig. The listings rerun as printed, and so do the exercise modifications I tried. Keynesian
material is labelled a toy model (ch. 27), government failure has its place (ch. 18, ch. 21 on rules, ch. 25 on
discretion), and the debate boxes are about evidence. The defects are in the data behind the records, the trap list's own
ground:
- back-filled or initial-condition series read as fact (Korea's PWT capital stock, R4-033; Germany before 1991, R4-027);
- a CPI from JST where BLS was required (R4-028);
- a real-growth figure that is per person (R4-030);
- an investment share at PPP set against a labour share at national prices (R4-032);
- war years leaking into a "war years left out" chart (R4-039).
One exercise solution makes a wrong dynamic claim (R4-035), and one problem question has its direction reversed (R4-025).
"Tested" fields that cite only consistency recur (R4-029, R4-031, R4-038). No chapter is thin.

**Part VI, Econometrics (chapters 30--34).** A sound first course: matrix OLS with the projection picture, FWL, the exact
OVB identity, Gauss--Markov with a proof, sandwich, cluster and HAC errors built by hand against statsmodels, potential
outcomes with the decomposition, and time series with spurious regression shown by simulation. Every listing reruns as
printed. Every exercise and problem number I recomputed matches: by hand, from the NSW, CPS, RAND, Engel and macrodata
files, from Brenland's survey and accounts, and through the chapter tests, which use a second route (statsmodels against
numpy normal equations) and are not self-confirming. The defects are small:
- one example's wording inverts the comparison (bad control, R4-045);
- a "three" that should be "four" (R4-042);
- normal and t critical values confused once (R4-046);
- a trimming explanation that the data contradict (R4-047);
- ratios printed from rounded inputs (R4-044, R4-049);
- one teaching gap worth fixing: the RAND plans were assigned by family, so the chapter's own Moulton lesson applies to
  its own example and is not said (R4-043).
The causal chapter is careful and neutral: LaLonde, STAR and Allcott are reported as the ledger states. No chapter is thin.
