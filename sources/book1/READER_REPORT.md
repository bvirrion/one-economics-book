## Overall judgement

**Counts.** 32 defects: **3 major** (R1-002, R1-006, R1-010), **17 minor**, **12 nits**. Every solution
in the book was recomputed along its printed, rounded path, and no solution gives a wrong final answer. The
two defects that come closest are R1-012 (an exercise asks for a size the model cannot give) and R1-024 (an
exercise asks the pupil to read from the text a value the text does not print). Every `% figdata:` number I
checked matches its CSV. All the charts looked at render correctly; R1-005 (a clipped peak) and R1-031 (a
label struck through) are the only drawing faults.

**Patterns across the book.**
1. **The `\tested` field often cites a chart that describes rather than tests.** See R1-010, R1-014, R1-017,
   R1-018, R1-026 and R1-029. In these boxes the "test" is either a picture that any model would fit (firms
   are born and close; export shares fall with country size; phone prices fell while subscriptions rose), or
   a case outside the model's own assumptions (a US tariff for the small-country model). The fix is cheap:
   say "consistent with", or point to the chapter where the real test is made.
2. **Food share of income against food share of spending** (R1-006, R1-017). The g6-06 chart, and the Engel
   box in g7-07 that relies on it, measure food against *pre-tax income*. In the bottom US fifth, spending is
   twice reported income, so that measure manufactures most of the fall. The spending shares in g7-07's own
   opening chart are almost flat. This is the most visible economics error, because it sits under a law the
   book calls one of the most reliable regularities.
3. **Invented-country consistency** (R1-016, R1-030). The income/transfers definitions double-count
   transfers. Kestria is half as rich as Brenland in g8-04 and as rich or richer in g9-05.

**Neutrality.** I read each chapter once as a reader on the left and once as a reader on the right.
- No school of thought, no Marx and no history of thought appear. Names appear only as author-year
  citations (Nordhaus 1996 has a characterising phrase, R1-003). Ricardo appears only in g7-03.
- Government failure sits next to market failure ("Who decides", g8-07). The tariff, rent-control and
  institutions chapters keep their `\breaks` fields honest (South Korea's large state role; growth where
  property was not secure; tenants' gain from rent control shown beside the supply cut).
- The one place where the wording leans is g9-03, where a model estimate is printed as a measured
  city-wide rent rise (R1-028).
- The trade-deficit paragraph (g9-04), the debt chapter (g8-07) and the distribution chapter (g9-07, "two
  facts together") are carefully balanced.
- No curriculum, school programme or university is named in print. References are to "the volume for
  grades 10 to 12" and so on. Nothing personal appears.

**Grade 6 (7 chapters).** Strong and deep: long-run charts, careful measure boxes, and weekend problems
that make the pupil work with real data. Level: right for 11-12-year-olds, with the times-ten scale taught
patiently. The main faults are R1-002 (the hook contradicts the chapter's own data) and R1-006 (the food
chart). **No thin chapter.**

**Grade 7 (8 chapters).** Solid, with good worked problems (bakery break-even, households A-E, juice
stall). The model boxes are where the flaws are: R1-010 (opportunity cost predicts *less* free time; "more
free time" is an unstated income effect), R1-013 and R1-014 (comparative advantage evidence overstated) and
R1-018. Level: right, and algebra-free as intended. **No thin chapter.** g7-04 Money and g7-06 Demand and
supply are especially clean.

**Grade 8 (8 chapters).** Very clean numerically: every figdata and ledger number I checked matches, and
every solution, including the compounding and incidence paths, is correct. Good use of long JST series
(inflation, unemployment, rates, debt). The faults are small: R1-019 (SNA 2008 without a date, now
superseded), R1-021 (a strange CPI basket), R1-022 (an ambiguous survey answer), R1-023 (1929 put down to a
panic) and R1-024 (an unanswerable exercise). Level: right. The jump to powers and the rule of 70 is well
scaffolded. **No thin chapter.**

**Grade 9 (7 chapters).** The affine supply-and-demand algebra and the tax-incidence formula are correct:
buyers bear t·d/(b+d), and the provenance comment rightly corrects the brief. Elasticity, trade, exchange
rates and distribution are well judged for 14-15-year-olds. Faults: R1-026 (the phones chart is not one
market), R1-028 (rent-control estimate), R1-030 (Kestria), and nits on tests and labels. The institutions
chapter handles a sensitive subject with natural experiments and an explicit "correlation, not yet a cause"
section. **No thin chapter.** g9-07 is a description chapter with no model box, as its brief intends.

**Thin chapters: none.** Every chapter has its opening chart (from grade 7 on), several definitions, at
least one measure box, 12 graded exercises with solutions and a 12-13-question weekend problem. The
lightest in course text are g7-08 Firms and g8-08 Competition, and both are still adequate for the level.
