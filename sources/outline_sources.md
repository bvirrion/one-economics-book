# Outline sources

Provenance of `OUTLINE.md` (written 2026-11-20). This is the only file, with
source comments, where the source programmes are named. The printed books never
name a programme, a country's school system or a university.

The lists below were written from knowledge of the programmes and of the
international textbook sequence, not from the official texts. **Before a book is
written, its chapter list is checked against the published programme texts**,
and each chapter brief records the items it covers.

## Book 2 — Grades 10–12

| Grade | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 10–12 | The economics half of SES (*sciences économiques et sociales*: seconde, première and terminale speciality): market and price formation, perfect and imperfect competition, market failures, money and financing, growth, international trade, unemployment, crises, European policy, environment | Kept: the economics of every item. Dropped: the sociology and political-science halves; the prescriptive chapter titles ("how to fight unemployment", "which inequalities are compatible with social justice", "what public action for the environment"), whose content is re-homed in `labour-market`, `unemployment`, `inequality`, `externalities`; the framing of the state as the fix |
| 10–12 | The international *principles* sequence (two semesters: microeconomics, macroeconomics), as taught in first university courses and in the advanced high-school courses of several countries | It sets the order and adds what the French course lacks or treats lightly: elasticities, surplus and deadweight loss, tax incidence, Ricardo's model with numbers, present value, the quantity theory, the Solow model, public choice |
| 11 | Added by this outline | `public-choice-first-look` (proposed 2026-11-19, confirmed 2026-11-20) |

Grade 10 has the microeconomics that needs no derivative, plus measurement.
Grade 11 starts with the derivative (firms, market structures), then market
failure, government failure, labour and money. Grade 12 has everything that
needs the exponential, the logarithm, sequences and their limits, or
expectation: present value, risk, growth, the short run, the open economy.

The mathematics, statistics theory and computing courses of the degree are not
in this series: *One Math Book* and *One Computer Science Book* own them. The
accounting, management and law courses that some degrees include are left out.

## What was dropped, in one list

- Sociology and political science (the other halves of SES and of the
  preparatory-class course).
- Every prescriptive framing; the content was kept under descriptive titles.
- The history of economic thought, and every school of thought taught as an
  alternative: Marxist economics, the regulation school, post-Keynesian
  economics, the economy of conventions, degrowth.
- Economic history as a subject. Dated episodes appear only as tests of a model
  (`record` boxes) or as data.
- Finance beyond present value, a first asset-pricing model and banking crises:
  the quant series owns it.
- Household advice: the planned personal finance book owns it.
